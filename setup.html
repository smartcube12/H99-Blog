4/3/25

Since I started this blog after I already did my first semester no one really knows how I got into being a TA so since the past few weeks have been slow, I figured I might as well spill. Picture me freshman year, hanging out with some sophomore friends of mine when one suggests I become a TA. I think nah that’s not for me, but he really encouraged me to do so. He pointed out that I already had most of the qualities from my time being choir section leader. I had to be dependable, knowledgeable on content, and an effective communicator. Plus, it’d be great job experience and a decent way to make connections. I thought about it but at the time it was the middle of the semester, so no teachers were looking at the time, but fast forward to the end of the semester and Dr. Bolman announces that she’s looking for 5 teaching assistants to help her in the spring semester when class enrollment is much higher. Naturally I figured that I wouldn’t get the job, but I emailed her about my interest anyways. From there its history, now I don’t remember all of the in-between there were some papers that had to be submitted and a quick interview but ultimately, I found myself through the whole process, completely hired and feeling underwhelmed. I was under the impression that it’d be much harder than that, but it was much simpler than I had imagined, and my friend was right. I took to being a TA like it was as natural as breathing.

3/8/25

We have just started grading homework 3. I personally planned to finish it before spring break and realistically I could still make that goal, but it just hit me now how much the class’s homework structure has changed. When I took the class there were 4 homework assignments spread throughout the semester averaging about 1 every 4 weeks. Now there are 6 homework assignments, all covering the same material, but spaced out so there’s less questions per assignment. In the grand scheme of things nothing has changed but it still feels weird to see them isolated to one or two topics at a time. Adjusting to the new change may take some time but I know it’s better for everyone involved. The students have less questions to worry about and I have less to grade at once, but it feels weird being the last to experience a system then being the one helping facilitate the new one.

2/18/25

It’s come to my attention that many of my fine readers don’t know what a TA does. I’ll be happy to explain it. In a typical week I hold office hours Thursday and Friday for at least two hours a week. I sit in the SRC and wait for students to come with questions about the class content or help with assignments. Typically, more students come in closer to deadlines which makes sense. The other duty you have is grading homework. Now it’s not too hard as we are all assigned a block of students to grade and a pretty forgiving deadline. If you were a graduate TA you would host recitation but most of you don’t have to worry about that and I have no experience leading it. The last responsibility you have as a TA is occasionally checking your email or Piazza for questions. With both you respond with help to the best of your ability but on Piazza all questions and answers are public since it’s much like a forum. That sums up all the responsibilities you have as an undergraduate TA, answer questions, hold office hours, and grade homework. Although this may seem easy it can be a little hectic if you don’t learn this vital skill. “What is this skill?” You may ask. It’s time management. This is the most important thing you will learn in any job but especially as a TA. You have to carve out time to sit in a room to help people weekly, periodically check for questions, and grade often times over 10 assignments. If you aren’t careful you could fall behind in your work or even your own schoolwork. This was me my first semester as a TA, I wasn’t careful and ended up struggling under a mountain of homework both to do and grade because I wasn’t being smart with how I spent my time. If you take away nothing from this, take away that learning proper time management skills is a must as a TA!
